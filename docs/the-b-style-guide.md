Copied as given by the newsroom, Nov. 23, 2026. This is the source for the house rule pack. See `house-rule-map.md` for how each item becomes a Buzzword rule.

Style Rules

In The B, we stick by style rules to ensure a consistent, understandable and stylish newspaper layout.  If rule is not specified, consult the AP stylebook and/or the copy editor and editors-in-chief.

The B Specific Rules:
- Always sophomore, junior, or senior; never "10th graders" or capitalized
- Referencing the school, just use Burlingame in print or online
- Full names upon first reference, then called by last names.  No Mr. or Mrs.
- For people in the same family, use full name on the first reference and then just their first name throughout.
- "UC Davis" upon first reference, then UCD.  Same goes for UC Santa Barbara (UCSB) and UC San Diego (UCSD). UCLA is always just UCLA (first and second reference). UC Berkeley is Berkeley or Cal on second reference. UC Irvine, UC Riverside, and UC Merced are not well enough known in our area to have a familiar second reference abbreviation. UC Santa Cruz is well known but people don't know what "UCSC" means so we don't use it.
- TheBurlingameB.org
- We have vice principals (not assistant principals, not vice-principals, not Vice Principals).
- We write in the past tense only—unless it's a feature article where present tense is used for a specific effect.
- Classes aren't capitalized unless it's the full name of a special class or the name of a language. Examples: AP Statistics, Spanish, math, biology, trigonometry, ROP Desktop Publishing.
- Room numbers are listed as the building letter and number (A112).
- No Oxford commas
- No exclamation marks
- It's "website" and "email"
- Associated Student Body (ASB) on first reference, ASB on future references
- ASB officers capitalize as titles (ex: ASB Vice President)
- Class officers capitalize as titles (ex: Sophomore Vice President)
- Class officers lowercase as descriptions (ex: Jane Smith, vice president of the sophomore class)

Formatting:
- When you type your article, make only ONE space after a period. Make all text, including headline and byline flush left (never center).
- Even if mentioned by full name in article, photo captions should still utilize full name (first and last)

Sports:
- IMPORTANT: Read the AP Style book sports section on your sport before writing your story. Every sport has its own rules. (SERIOUSLY! DO IT!)
- Men's and women's instead of boy's and girl's. Don't forget the apostrophe!
- When discussing a band, team, or other group of students, be sure to refer to the group as "it" and not "them," as the group itself is singular. WRONG: The team played their best. There are two ways to correct it: The team played its best. / Team members played their best.
- Varsity is never capitalized. JV is used, not junior varsity and not J.V.
- Blue Devils and Devils are always capitalized (but there's no apostrophe!)
- Head coach, coach and assistant coach titles must always be lower case even if the title is before a name. BAD: "I hate those sophomore boys," baseball Head Coach Dan Ariola said. GOOD: "The senior class is so mature," baseball head coach Dan Ariola said.
- Never use abbreviations like PR or SB. Write out personal record or season best.
- Other schools' mascot names can be used only if the school was mentioned previously. Capitalize other schools'/teams' mascots names.
- Rules for photo captions: All players must be identified by full name. Descriptions are written in present tense. Can't copy information in headline or article.

Quotes:
- Use quotes to add opinion or voice. Do not use quotes just to convey facts that could be better summarized by the reporter.
- Speech tags: After a quote, it's always subject-verb unless the subject has a long describer. WRONG: "I hate the world," said Lee. RIGHT: "I hate the world," Lee said. Exception: a really long title: "I love people," said Queen of All Things Rad Kacey Hsu. Sophomore, junior, senior or teacher's department do not count as long describers: "I love people," senior Will Bodendorfer said. / "I am failing everybody," journalism teacher Kelly Wilkerson said.
- Quotation marks go AFTER a comma or a period!
- Only put one quotation per paragraph. New person = new paragraph.
- In almost all cases, it's "said" not "says." Exception: a feature or column using present tense for a "you-are-there" style.
- Words like retorted, explained, replied, blurted, noted, confirmed, smiled, lamented, etc. inject OPINION into articles. "Said" or "says" are always right.
- Don't back into quotes, or inject the reporter or question into the text. WRONG: When asked about the budget cuts, Brown said they would be devastating. RIGHT: Brown said the budget cuts would be "devastating."

Common Mistakes (The B follows the AP Stylebook):
- Adviser, NOT "advisor."
- Ages: always figures, unless the first word of a sentence (then spell out or rewrite). The 5-year-old girl. The girl, 10. The man is 32 years old.
- Composition titles: no italics or underline; quotation marks around books, computer games, movies, plays, poems, songs, TV programs, works of art. Not the Bible or reference books.
- Contractions: generally avoid them (OK in informal contexts reflecting speech).
- Dates: figures, no st/nd/rd/th. Omit the year if within 12 months of publication. Abbreviate months with more than five letters (July, Oct.). Never use a day of the week with a date (NO: On Tuesday, Sept. 5…).
- Numerals: spell out one through nine, figures for 10 or more; many exceptions.
- Percent: figure + the word "percent" (1 percent, 16 percent). Don't use the "%" symbol. Spell out at the start of a sentence.
- Time: figures, no ":00" on the hour (10 a.m., 5 p.m.); "5 o'clock in the afternoon" OK; colon for minutes (10:01 a.m.). Avoid redundancies ("5 p.m. in the afternoon"). 12 p.m. → "noon", 12 a.m. → "midnight". Don't capitalize a.m./p.m.
- Titles: capitalize formal titles directly before a name; lowercase after a name (set off with commas) or standing alone; don't capitalize job descriptions (custodian, teacher); omit titles on second reference; "Dr." only for medical doctors.

Common Grammar Mistakes:
- their / they're / there
- affect (verb) vs. effect (noun)
- it's vs. its
- your vs. you're
- who (people, named animals) vs. that (objects, unnamed animals)
- Apostrophes only for possessives, not plurals: UCs, 1960s (not UC's, 1960's)
- "since" for time, "because" for reasons
- "over" is a preposition; use "more than" with numbers ("won more than 20 games")
- Never begin a sentence with a numeral, except years.
- Buildings or districts can't do anything; only people can (WRONG: DJUSD plans to cut programs).
